## 1. Summary in plain terms

Plan 1b found a clear break in the state data: population growth stopped following income. It couldn't say when.
- **The tie:** the best fit was 1990, but resampling split between 1990 (37%) and 1970 (30%).
- **What points to 1970:** without the oil states, or with income standardized within year, the best fit is 1970.

This plan asks the question directly: **during 1970–1989, did states behave like before 1970 or like after 1990?** There are four possible answers:

- **1970:** 1970–89 already looks like "after". The change came around 1970.
- **1990:** 1970–89 still looks like "before". The change came around 1990.
- **Gradual:** 1970–89 sits clearly in between. The change was a slide, not a single break. That would also explain why the two candidate years tie.
- **Can't tell:** the data doesn't separate these.

The rule that decides between them is fixed in §4.1 before anything is run.

Three things could blur the answer, and each gets its own check. None is assumed to push in a particular direction.

1. **Regional booms and busts.** The 1973–1990 oil cycle, the 1980s farm crisis, the Rust Belt collapse. These moved whole groups of states up and down together. **Check:** separate year effects for the oil states during the oil years (the main version), and a version that compares states only within their own region each year.
2. **Births and deaths.** Total growth = people moving + births − deaths. Birth-rate gaps between richer and poorer states changed over the period. That could hide a change in moving or fake one. **Check:** split the decade result exactly into a moving part (net migration) and a births-minus-deaths part.
3. **The 1965 immigration law.** Immigration rose sharply after it, mostly into a few rich gateway states. That raises growth in rich states after 1970 for reasons unrelated to Americans' own moving. **Check:** the net-migration tests without the six main gateway states.

**What success looks like:** one sentence for the paper. It names the answer (1970, 1990, gradual, or can't tell), shows how far 1970–89 had moved toward "after" with a range, and says whether moving or births drive it.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
- A three-period test on the annual state panel (1929–2024), with the oil terms, against a decision rule fixed in advance.
- A state net-migration panel by decade (1950s–2000s, plus the 2010s if available), with the same three-period test and an exact split of the result into moving vs. births minus deaths.
- An updated RQ0b finding.

**Non-goals**
- **Census mover microdata (IPUMS):** saved for later, as the paper's confirmation step.
- **No yearly net migration.** It would start in 1968, too late to see 1970, and Plan 1b's 1969+ state test already covers the years after 1970.
- **No 1940s net migration.** The war years are distorted by troop moves, and the "before" period has two decades without it.
- **No typing up of the 1939–1967 birth and death tables** (option B in the 2026-10-15 discussion).
- **No metro version.** Metro income changes source exactly in 1970 (Plan 1), so a metro 1970 test is confounded.
- **No new causal claims.** RQ0b stays descriptive.

  - total growth `G = (P(d+10) − P(d)) / P(d)`;

## 4. Methods

### 4.1 The three-period test and the decision rule (the headline)

On the annual state growth panel (Plan 1b §4.2), fix two cut years and let the income slope differ across three periods:

`growth(i,t) = α_c(i,t) + β_pre · x·[t < 1970] + β_mid · x·[1970 ≤ t < 1990] + β_post · x·[t ≥ 1990] + e(i,t)`

- **The cell effects α_c.** The main version uses cells = year, plus year × energy group during the oil years (1973–1990). The oil states thereby get their own year effects in those years, and the oil cycle can't show up in β.
  - Fast estimation from Plan 1b carries over. Each cell lies inside one period, so demeaning within cells removes these effects exactly. The demeaning step takes a cell column instead of the year.
- **The headline number is "how far 1970–89 had moved toward after":**

  `share = (β_pre − β_mid) / (β_pre − β_post)`

  - 0 means 1970–89 looks exactly like before; 1 means exactly like after.
  - Its 90% range comes from resampling states (999 draws, as in Plan 1b), which keeps each state's years together.
- **The decision rule (fixed before running):**

  | 90% range of `share` (low, high) | Answer |
  |---|---|
  | low > 0 and high ≥ 1 | **1970** |
  | low ≤ 0 and high < 1 | **1990** |
  | low > 0 and high < 1 | **Gradual** (or a step between the two) |
  | low ≤ 0 and high ≥ 1 | **Can't tell** |

  The four cases cover every possible range, and no range falls into two of them. A range that lies wholly above 1 (1970–89 already past "after") counts as 1970; one wholly below 0 counts as 1990.

  - **Guard:** the rule applies only if the overall change is real, meaning β_pre and β_post differ significantly (clustered Wald, 5%). Otherwise the answer is "can't tell".
  - **Fragility:** the main version sets the answer. If any robustness version below gives a different answer, the finding is reported as "fragile", with the versions listed.
- **Also reported, for context:**
  - the three β's (one-year scale and ×10);
  - clustered Wald tests of β_mid = β_pre and of β_mid = β_post;
  - Plan 1b's single-break search re-run with the oil terms, with its resampling vote shares compared with 37% / 30%.
  - These are descriptive. The rule above decides the answer.
- **Robustness:**
  - no oil terms (as in Plan 1b);
  - the hand-picked energy list;
  - a shorter oil window (1973–1986, boom to price collapse);
  - **within-region:** cells = year × census division throughout, so states are compared only with their own region each year. That removes every regional shock at once (oil, farm crisis, Rust Belt). It answers a narrower question: does growth follow income *within* a region?
  - income standardized within year;
  - without the WWII years (1940–1946).

### 4.2 Net migration by decade

- **The same three-period test on the decade panel.**
  - Outcome: the decade net-migration rate M, with decade fixed effects.
  - Periods: before = the 1950s and 1960s; middle = the 1970s and 1980s; after = the 1990s on.
  - Same `share`, same range method, same decision rule. This shows whether the answer holds for moving itself.
- **Splitting the result exactly.** Total growth = moving + births minus deaths (G = M + N), and each β is a linear least-squares fit on the same states, income and fixed effects. So:

  `β_G = β_M + β_N`

  holds exactly for every decade and every period, and the change in the total pull splits exactly into a moving part and a births-minus-deaths part.
  - Table: per period, β_G, β_M, β_N.
  - Figure: decade β for total growth, net migration and natural increase.
  - If the change in β_G comes mostly from β_M, the RQ0b finding is about moving. If β_N carries a large part, births were distorting it (in either direction).
- **Robustness:**
  - without the six main gateway states for immigrants (CA, NY, NJ, IL, FL, TX);
  - without the energy states;
  - log income standardized within decade.

### 4.3 What gets reported

- **One table,** `reports/tables/rq0b_1970_vs_1990.csv`, with columns `analysis, outcome, spec, beta_pre, beta_mid, beta_post, share, share_low, share_high, answer, detail`. One row per version in §4.1 and §4.2.
- **One small split table,** `reports/tables/rq0b_growth_split.csv`, giving β_G, β_M and β_N by period and by decade.
- **One figure,** `reports/figures/rq0b_growth_split.png`.
- **A new notebook 1.1 section,** "1970 or 1990?", ending with a plain finding that states the rule's answer first.

## 6. Testing

- **Synthetic recovery (fast)**, on annual panels like Plan 1b's test panels:

  | Planted | `decide` must give |
  |---|---|
  | a step at 1970 | "1970", share near 1 |
  | a step at 1990 | "1990", share near 0 |
  | a straight-line slide from 1970 to 1990 | "gradual" |
  | no change (β_pre = β_post) | "can't tell" (the guard) |
  | a step at 1970 plus a boom-and-bust episode in 8 of 50 units (1973–1990) | without the oil terms, share is pulled away from 1; with them, "1970" |

- **Exactness:**
  - Demeaning by cells gives the same β's, SSR and clustered Wald as a regression with explicit dummies, to 1e-8.
  - On a toy decade panel, `β_G = β_M + β_N` holds to 1e-10.
- **Data tests:**
  - **Coverage:** counties matched to states cover at least 99.5% of US population in each decade.
  - **Natural increase:** the national sum of N × P per decade is within 5% of the published US births minus deaths for that decade. Those values are entered in `references/known_values.csv` with their source.
  - **Net migration sums to immigration:** domestic moves cancel out across states, so the sum of state net migration per decade must be positive and within 25% of the published US net international migration for that decade. Those values go in `references/known_values.csv` with their source.
  - **Population agrees:** the APL's own base populations, where included, match `us_state_longrun` within 0.5%.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The APL/ICPSR files don't cover a decade, or need registration | Task 1 confirms first. A missing 2010s leaves the "after" period as the 1990s and 2000s. A missing 1950s would leave only one "before" decade, and the decade test is then reported as weak |
| The share is unstable when β_pre and β_post are close | The guard in §4.1. Plan 1b's gap (0.165 vs. −0.028 per decade) is large |
| Oil or division cells absorb too much variation (8 energy states; about 5 states per division) | Reported as robustness, not the main version. Fewer states per cell widens the range rather than biasing it |
| "1970" and "1990" are fixed cut years, while the truth could be 1972 or 1987 | The rule asks about the whole 1970–89 period, which is robust to a few years either way; the single-break search re-run is reported alongside |
| Retiree moves to the Sunbelt (toward climate, not income) grew after 1970 | Stated as an interpretive caveat. The division version partly absorbs it |
| The APL estimates are themselves built from census counts and vital statistics | That's the standard method. The natural-increase data test checks the pieces against published national totals |

## 8. Decisions for the user

2. **Decision rule:** the 90% range of `share` decides, as in §4.1, fixed before running. Recommended.
3. **Oil window:** 1973–1990 main, 1973–1986 as a check. Recommended.

## 10. Changes from the first draft (design review, 2026-10-15)

1. **Headline test:** the BIC comparison of 1970 vs. 1990 was replaced by the three-period test. With about 4,800 correlated state-years, a "BIC gap ≥ 10" would appear for tiny differences in fit.
2. **Decision rule:** added and fixed in advance (§4.1), including "gradual" as a possible answer.
3. **Direction of the confounds:** no longer assumed. The oil cycle can push either way: the boom raises the measured pull, the bust lowers it. The 1980s rebound in ten-year β (+0.21 for windows starting in 1980) can't come from oil. Hence the within-division check for all regional shocks.
4. **Births:** an exact split β_G = β_M + β_N replaces a test that assumed the baby boom faked a break. Richer states had lower birth rates, so births could equally have hidden a change.
5. **The 1965 immigration law:** added as a third confound, with a six-gateway-state check (the draft dropped four states).
6. **Data:** county net-migration estimates by decade from the APL replace about 200 hand-entered Census numbers and a new birth and death data source. The yearly 1968+ series and the 1940s were dropped (§2).
7. **Growth measure:** simple percent change instead of log growth, so the split is exact.
